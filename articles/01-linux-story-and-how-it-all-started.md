In 1991, a university student posted a simple message on the internet:



> \*“I’m doing a free operating system… just a hobby.”\*



That student was \*\*Linus Torvalds\*\*.



What started as a small hobby project eventually became Linux — the operating system that now powers most of the internet, cloud infrastructure, supercomputers, and even billions of smartphones.





\## Introduction



Linux is one of the most powerful, free, and widely used operating systems in the world.



But the story of \*\*Linux started with a simple experiment by a university student.\*\*





\## The First Announcement



Linux was developed by \*\*Linus Torvalds\*\* with contributions from the global open-source community and was first announced by Linus in a post he made on \*\*August 25, 1991\*\*.



At the time, he was experimenting with building his own operating system kernel as a personal project. What started as a small experiment soon attracted developers from around the world.





\## Why Linux Became Popular?



Linux became widely popular because it is \*\*free and open-source\*\*. This means developers can access the entire source code, modify it, and improve the system.



Because of this openness:



\- Developers can add new features easily.

\- Bugs can be identified and fixed quickly.

\- The community can continuously improve the system.



Today, Linux is used by millions of users worldwide, including individuals, universities, research centers, and large companies that see Linux as a powerful alternative to other operating systems.





\## Linux Distributions



Over time, different communities created their own versions of Linux called \*\*distributions (distros)\*\*.



Some popular Linux distributions include:



\- Debian

\- Ubuntu

\- Gentoo

\- Puppy Linux

\- Damn Small Linux

\- Chrome OS



Each distribution is designed for different types of users, from beginners to advanced developers.





\## Conclusion



Linux started as a simple hobby project but evolved into one of the most important technologies in the modern world.



Today, millions of developers and companies contribute to Linux, making it a powerful and constantly evolving operating system.
